\section{Lessons for Practitioners}

\paragraph{Benchmark accuracy is the wrong selection criterion.} The most accurate classifier on the
benchmark was the least accurate a year later and on a new repository. Evaluate a labeler on issues
from after its training and calibration data, and on a held-out repository, before choosing it.

\paragraph{A calibrated threshold is not an error guarantee.} Tuning the threshold to the target on a
few hundred calibration issues over-promised in every condition. Use a risk-controlling procedure, and
budget for thousands of labeled calibration issues if the target should be tight and coverage useful.
Recalibrate when a repository is onboarded; no threshold carried over.

\paragraph{Defer, and the rewording problem mostly goes away.} Labels flip under harmless rewording,
and a newcomer's apology pushes the LLM toward \emph{question}, but those flips concentrate where the
service is unsure. Deferral is a fairness mechanism as much as an accuracy one, provided that deferred
issues are actually reviewed.

\paragraph{Show evidence as context, not as cause.} The LLM's quotes are genuine but only weakly
faithful; a triage UI should present them as relevant passages, not as the reason for the label.

\paragraph{Monitor inputs, not only the model.} A nearest-neighbor novelty score over the labeled
history caught a new repository within a dozen requests; the model's confidence did not.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\paragraph{Construct.} Labels are the repositories' own type labels, which mix issue content with
project policy (React routes questions elsewhere). The 2025--26 set maps each repository's label names
to the three types; issues with several type labels are excluded. Rewrites are synthetic: the
non-native rewrite was validated automatically (code blocks restored, length and technical tokens
preserved; 161 of 210 valid) and spot-checked by hand, and occasionally alters template fields.

\paragraph{Internal.} The calibration split comes from the benchmark's training half and the test set
from its test half, as in the competition; thresholds are fixed before evaluation. Probabilities are
read from the first output token; a different prompt or label wording would change them.

\paragraph{External.} One LLM, five large repositories, three labels. Smaller projects, other label
sets, and stronger models (including fine-tuned encoders such as the SetFit competition baseline)
may behave differently. All runs used one laptop.

\paragraph{Conclusion validity.} Rewording tests use 77--210 variants per kind; differences of a few
points between classifiers are within noise, while the newcomer effect on the few-shot LLM is not.

\section{Conclusion}
Treating issue triage as a deployed, selective service changes what matters. The LLM was less
accurate than a simple classifier on the benchmark but held up a year later and on a new repository;
none of the classifiers could keep a promised error rate under drift without either over-promising or
deferring almost everything; harmless rewording and a newcomer's apology flipped labels, mostly among
issues the service would hand off anyway; and the cheapest monitor, input novelty, was the most useful.
The harness, data collection scripts, recorded model scores and regression suite are available for
replication.
